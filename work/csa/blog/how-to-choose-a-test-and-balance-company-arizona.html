How to Choose a Test & Balance Company in Arizona

A short, direct checklist for evaluating TAB proposals — the questions that actually separate a credentialed, accountable firm from a low bid with nothing behind it.

Test and balance proposals can look nearly identical on paper — a list of services, a price, a timeline. The differences that actually matter are underneath that surface, and most of them can be checked in a five-minute phone call.

Start With Certification, Not Price

Before comparing a single dollar figure, ask every firm on your shortlist for their NEBB or AABC registration number. This is public information, and any legitimate firm will provide it immediately. A firm that can't, or that answers vaguely ("we follow industry standards"), should be treated as a red flag regardless of how competitive their bid is — because the number on the bottom of a low-cost proposal means nothing if the process producing it isn't certified.

Ask About the Warranty

Ask directly: "What warranty do you provide on this work, and for how long?" Across the Arizona market, this question exposes a real gap — most firms don't publish a warranty on test and balance work at all. Commercial Systems Analysis is the only Arizona TAB firm offering a full two-year warranty on every project, plus a mandatory one-year quality assurance warranty on any annually certified system. If a firm hesitates on this question, that tells you something about their confidence in their own process.

Check Whether the Scope Actually Fits

Not every TAB firm covers every discipline. Some specialize narrowly in air balance; others cover the full range including life safety testing, critical room certification, duct leak testing, stairwell pressurization, kitchen hood certification, and commissioning. Before hiring, map your building's actual needs against the firm's published scope — a hospital wing needs a firm that does critical room certification; a high-rise needs one that tests stairwell pressurization. Don't assume a general TAB firm covers specialty disciplines unless they say so explicitly.

Get Real, Checkable References

Ask for specific, named projects — not a generic client list. A firm with a real track record should be able to point to actual buildings: hospitals, high-rises, university facilities, government buildings. Years in continuous operation matters here too; a firm that's been balancing buildings in Arizona for decades has had more opportunities to be tested by inspectors, engineers, and re-certification cycles than one that opened last year.

The Short Version

  • Get the NEBB or AABC registration number, in writing, before signing anything.
  • Ask specifically what warranty covers the work, and for how long.
  • Confirm the firm's published scope actually covers what your building needs.
  • Ask for named, checkable project references — not a generic client list.
  • Favor firms with a long, continuous operating history in your state.
