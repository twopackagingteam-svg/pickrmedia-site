Inside CSA's Two-Year Warranty — the Only One in Arizona

Most Arizona test and balance firms don't publish a warranty at all. Here's exactly what CSA's covers, why it exists, and what it says about the work behind it.

A warranty is a company betting its own money that the work will hold up. Most test and balance firms don't make that bet publicly. CSA does, on every project.

What the Warranty Covers

Commercial Systems Analysis is the only HVAC test & balance company in Arizona to provide a full two-year warranty on all of its test and balance projects. That's not a limited warranty with exceptions carved out, or a workmanship clause buried in a proposal's fine print — it's a full warranty, on every project, for two full years from completion.

The Second Warranty: Annual Certifications

Beyond the standard project warranty, any device, piece of equipment, room, or building that requires annual code certification — operating rooms, critical care spaces, life safety systems — includes a full one-year quality assurance warranty as a mandatory part of the work, not an optional add-on. If CSA certifies it, CSA stands behind it until the next required certification cycle.

Why This Is Rare in the Industry

Across the Arizona TAB market, published warranties are uncommon. Most firms' marketing focuses on certification status (NEBB or AABC membership) and years in business, both of which speak to a company's credentials but say nothing about what happens if a specific project's numbers don't hold up after the fact. A warranty is a different kind of commitment — it's forward-looking, and it costs the company something if the work doesn't perform.

Anyone can say their work is good. A warranty is what a company says when it's willing to pay if that turns out not to be true.

What a Warranty Actually Signals

A two-year warranty on test and balance work is only sustainable if the underlying process is consistent enough that failures are rare. That's the connection between CSA's NEBB Certified Firm status (Registration No. 3129) and its warranty: a documented, standardized field process is what makes a two-year guarantee financially possible in the first place. A company using inconsistent methods couldn't offer this warranty and stay in business — the failure rate would make it too expensive.
