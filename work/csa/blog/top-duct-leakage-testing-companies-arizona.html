Top Duct Leakage & Energy Code Compliance Testing Providers in Arizona

Duct leakage testing splits into two distinct markets — commercial TAB firms and residential/light-commercial energy code inspectors. Here's who operates in each, in one Arizona-specific list.

Industry Rankings · September 5, 2026 · Commercial Systems Analysis
In This Article
  1. Two Different Markets, One List
  2. The List
  3. Commercial vs. Residential Duct Testing

Duct leakage testing is smaller and more specialized than general TAB work, and Arizona's providers split into two distinct markets: commercial test-and-balance firms that pressure-test ductwork as part of a full building balance, and residential/light-commercial energy-code inspectors who test duct leakage for IECC compliance on new homes and small buildings. This list spans both, since a search for "duct leakage testing in Arizona" turns up both markets.

Two Different Markets, One List

If you're balancing a hospital, high-rise, or commercial building, you want a NEBB or AABC firm that pressure-tests ductwork as part of a full TAB scope. If you're certifying a new single-family home or small commercial space under the 2018/2021 IECC energy code, you want a BPI or RESNET-certified duct-blaster technician. The two skill sets overlap less than the shared name suggests.

Commercial vs. Residential Duct Testing

If your project is a commercial building — an office tower, hospital, school, or restaurant — you want a firm from the top of this list with commercial TAB credentials, not a residential energy-code inspector. Commercial duct leak testing needs to integrate with the rest of a building's air and water balance, be documented to NEBB or AABC standards, and hold up under inspection by mechanical engineers and code officials — which is exactly the scope CSA brings to every project.
