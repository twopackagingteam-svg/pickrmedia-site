HVAC Test & Balance

The heart of what we do. Using a full complement of calibrated instruments, our technicians test and adjust the air and water moving through your building's systems until measured performance matches design specification — outlet by outlet, coil by coil.

A properly balanced system does more than keep occupants comfortable. It reduces strain on fans, pumps, and compressors, cuts maintenance costs, extends equipment life, and drives down energy consumption for the life of the building. An unbalanced system quietly does the opposite — every hour it runs.

Critical Room & O.R. Certification

In a healthcare facility, air movement is a clinical variable. Operating rooms and critical care spaces require yearly testing and certification of their ventilation — and the margin for error is zero.

We verify the measurements that keep patients safe: pressure relationships between spaces, air changes per hour, and overall ventilation performance. Positive pressure keeps contaminants out of an operating room; negative pressure keeps them contained in isolation. Getting those relationships right — and proving it annually — is what keeps a healthcare facility compliant and its patients protected. Our healthcare portfolio includes facilities like Flagstaff Medical and San Carlos Hospital.

Duct Leak Testing

You paid to condition that air — leaky ductwork gives it away to your ceiling plenum. By pressurizing the duct system, we prove whether it was installed and sealed correctly.

Duct leakage testing verifies energy-efficiency compliance and documents the results, so leaks get found and fixed while they're still fixable — not after years of wasted energy.

Pressurized TestingInstallation VerificationEnergy Code Compliance
08

Stairwell Pressurization

In a high-rise fire, the stairwell is the way out — and code requires it to stay smoke-free. Pressurization systems hold the stairwell at a higher pressure than surrounding floors so smoke can't follow occupants in.

We test that the pressurization performs as designed and that stairwell doors still operate properly against that pressure — because an exit route only counts if people can actually use it.

High-Rise Code RequirementSmoke ControlDoor Operation
